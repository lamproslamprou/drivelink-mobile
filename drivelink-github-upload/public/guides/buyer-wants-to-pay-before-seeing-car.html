Selling privately

What to do when a buyer wants to pay before seeing the car

It is not automatically a scam — remote buyers are real, particularly for enthusiast and specialty vehicles. But an upfront payment you did not ask for is the single most common opening move in seller-side fraud, so it needs handling rather than accepting.

Last updated 17 August 2026

The message usually reads well. The buyer is polite, specific about the car, and offers to pay in full immediately without haggling. For a seller who has spent three weeks fielding lowball offers and no-shows, it is a relief.

That relief is what the fraudulent version is buying.

When it is genuine

Remote purchases happen constantly and legitimately, particularly for:

  • Enthusiast, collector, or specialty vehicles where the specific car matters more than convenience
  • Models that are scarce in the buyer's region
  • Buyers who have already paid for an independent inspection at a shop near you
  • Buyers who cannot travel easily but are genuinely purchasing for themselves

A genuine remote buyer behaves in recognisable ways. They ask detailed questions about the vehicle rather than about the payment. They want a video walkthrough, service records, and photos of specific things. They usually want an inspection and are willing to pay for it. They are relaxed about timing and entirely comfortable with you confirming funds before the car moves.

When it is not

The fraudulent version has a consistent shape. Any one of these is worth pausing on. Two together means stop.

SignalWhat it usually means
Barely asks about the carThey are not buying a car. They are running a payment.

The rule that covers every version

Never send money back through a different channel from the one it arrived in. Not to a shipper, not for an overpayment, not for a cancelled deal. This single rule defeats the entire overpayment family of scams.

How to structure it so you are safe either way

You do not need to decide whether the buyer is honest. You need a structure where it does not matter.

  1. Set the payment method yourself. This is your sale. State how you accept payment before discussing logistics, and do not move off it because the buyer prefers something else. A genuine buyer will not care much.
  2. Confirm funds with your own bank, not with the buyer. Not a screenshot, not a confirmation email, not a transaction reference. Log in and look, or call your bank.
  3. Do not release the car or the title until that confirmation exists. Including to a transporter. A transporter arriving early is not a reason to release; it is a reason to be suspicious.
  4. Offer an inspection instead of a deposit. If a buyer wants commitment, invite them to pay for an independent inspection at a shop of their choosing. It is a real signal of intent and it costs you nothing.
  5. Keep the whole negotiation in writing. If it goes wrong, the thread is what you will need.

What to say

You do not need to accuse anyone. A neutral line handles it:

"Happy to sell remotely — I do this through escrow so we are both covered. Funds are held by a third party, you get the car and title, then the money is released. Works the same for both of us."

A real buyer says fine. A fraudulent one produces reasons why escrow will not work, suggests a different service and sends you a link, or stops replying. Any of those three is your answer, and you have lost nothing.
